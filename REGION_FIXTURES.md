# Region fixtures — structures and interactables that carry each mood

[MOOD.md](MOOD.md) says what each region should make the player feel. [WORLD_MAP.md](WORLD_MAP.md) says where it sits. This file says **what is physically there**: the structures seen from the road, the one destination, the things a hand can touch, the marks on the ground, and the sounds that happen once in a long while. It is a design catalogue, not an authorization. Nothing here is built until a roadmap entry commissions it, and every item must pass the checks in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md#verification-and-completion). [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) still governs: one player, one coupe, no traffic, no people, no recent human activity, no music, no campfires, no theatrical camera.

## How to read a fixture

Every item is chosen by one test: **who used this, and what did they miss or lose?** A fixture earns its place when the player can feel an absence through wear, not through a note. Prefer a worn edge to a written explanation, a dead mechanism to a story, and a sound that stops to a sound that plays.

### Two kinds of place

[WORLD_LORE.md](WORLD_LORE.md) allows one new visitable place per region. To make a region rich without breaking that rule, this file separates:

- **Set pieces.** Structures passed on the road or at a pullout. The player can walk up to them and use their exterior or a single open threshold, but they have no furnished interior and are not destinations. They make the road worth driving.
- **The destination.** The one enterable, furnished place, with its own interior and a warm light.

### The interaction grammar

These apply to every interactable below, matching the existing station and motel conventions.

1. **Input.** `E` or click when the fixture is in reach and under the aim point. The HUD names it. Walking away ends it.
2. **Local and reversible.** Each action does one small thing at the object, can be undone, and leaves no puzzle, score, or collectible. State persists for the session.
3. **Sound first.** Every action has a distinct physical sound. A visual change is small and local.
4. **Five verbs.** Touch (wear), Listen (sound), Look (a view or a reading), Carry (a small item, at most one at a time), Operate (a switch, crank, lamp, or latch). Each region should use at least three.
5. **Quiet camera.** A close view may lean in while held and returns on release. No shake, no zoom pulse, no slow-motion.
6. **No recent human activity.** Every note, label, or mark is old and faded. Nothing is fresh.
7. **No hidden requirement.** A player can walk past every interactable and the region still works.

### Budget per region

About 5–7 set pieces, one destination, 8–12 interactables, 6–10 ground traces, and 3–5 sound events. Batch set pieces by spatial region, and measure the whole frame as in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). Counts are targets, not quotas. Cut an item rather than dilute the mood.

## West

### Cinder field — bracing aftermath

*The land burned once and went quiet. Everything here is hard, dark, and close, and everything old holds a little warmth.*

**Set pieces**

| Structure | What it is | What it evokes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| County cinder pit | A cone with a haul road cut into its side, a rusted loading hopper, and a bin of road cinder | Work that stopped. The land was mined for its own ash. |
| Lava-tube mouth | A pipe-railed opening in the black rock, with a chained gate | A slow, cool breath from the earth. Something older than the road. |
| Highway department sand shed | A dark tin lean-to, doors sagging, a grader blade rusting against it | The people who kept the road open, long gone. |
| Bent cattle guard | Rails sprung, one tie cracked | The fence line that no longer holds anything. |
| Whitewashed rock border | Black stones along a pullout, painted white, flaking | Someone's care, fading to the colour of the rock. |
| Culvert head | A stone-faced concrete mouth with a hand-painted mile | A small structure doing a job in the dark. |

**Destination: the cut-rock lunch counter** *(working name: Black Rock Lunch)*. A low stone-faced counter built into a notch in the cinder cone, with a tin roof and a single lit window. Inside: six stools, a short counter, a pass-through to a cold kitchen, a pie case, a menu board with push-in letters, a coffee urn, a dead jukebox, and a back wall that is raw rock.

| Fixture | Action and feedback | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm stone wall | Hold to lean a hand on the rock. It is still warm at dusk and goes cold through blue hour. The HUD reads "still warm", then "cold". | The land gives back what the sun gave it, and then stops. |
| Stool | Quarter-turn. A dry bearing and a worn arc on the floor. | Someone sat here and turned toward the road. |
| Pie case | Lift the dome. A squeak, then a plate with a faint ring where a slice sat. | An offering that ended. |
| Coffee urn | Turn the spigot. A dry cough of air, then nothing. | Ordinary warmth, gone. |
| Dead jukebox | Press a selector. The drum turns and clunks. The title strips are faded to blank. No sound plays. | Music is absent, and the machine still wants to. |
| Pass-through | Slide the sash. A wooden rattle and a view of a dark kitchen. | The other side of a counter you never stood behind. |
| Menu board | Look closely. Push-in letters, some missing, prices in cents. | Meals priced for a time that ended. |
| Window lamp | Operate the switch. The one lit window dims and returns. | One light is the whole shelter. |
| Lava-tube mouth | Stand at the gate. A cool draught, a slow drip, your own breath echoing back. | The earth breathes out, and the road is a small thing. |
| Sand shed door | Lift the latch and look in: a grader blade, a shovel on a nail, a cone of cinder. | The work is still waiting. |

**Ground and small traces:** cinder drifted across the asphalt in soft fans; windscreen glass glittering in the gravel when headlights pass; a brake drum planter holding dead creosote; tally scratches on a stone post; a faded orange rag on a survey stake; a ridge of grey ash under the sand shed eaves; black dust on the white border stones.

**Sound events:** rock ticking as it cools after sunset (the region's signature); a tin-roof ping as it contracts; a low whistle from the lava tube; a gust that lifts a little cinder across the road.

**Evening:** dusk catches orange on the black rock like an ember that will not go out; blue hour drains it to coal; at night only the window and the stars have colour.

**Considered and rejected:** glowing lava, ember particles, steam vents. Any of them reads as fire and breaks the quiet.

**Set pieces**

| Structure | What it is | What it evokes |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Starter's post | A rusted flag stand with a stained cloth | The gesture that began each run. |
| Bathtub rings | Pale terraces on the hills | A shoreline written on the land. |
| Car seat | A bench seat set on the shore, facing the flat | A place to wait and look at nothing. |

**Destination: the timing hut.** A plywood shack on low stilts with a roof deck, dark. The door is open. Inside: a bench with a stopwatch rail, a chalkboard of columns of times, a wall clock stopped, a field telephone, a tin cup, and an unlit kerosene lantern on a nail.

| Fixture | Action and feedback | Feeling |

| Stopwatch | Press to start. A mechanical tick. Press again to stop and read the dial. | You are timing nothing, and the number still matters. |
| Chalkboard | Lean close. Columns of times in different hands, none dated. | A small history of other people's speed. |
| Wall clock | Look. Stopped at a minute with no meaning. | Time that ended in this room. |
| Field telephone | Turn the crank. A dry ratchet and no line. | A call that nobody will answer. |
| Starter's flag | Raise or lower the flag. A cloth snap in the thin wind. | A signal given to an empty course. |
| Jetty end | Walk to the last plank. The pan drops a half-metre below, crusted white. | Standing where the water used to be. |
| Salt crust | Crouch. A close view of polygon cracks and tiny shells. | The land remembers being a sea. |
| Car seat | Sit. The horizon and sky blur into one pale field. | A place to wait for something that is not coming. |
| Tin cup | Pick up and set down. A single dull ring. | A drink someone once needed. |

**Ground and small traces:** polygon cracks in the crust; old tyre scars baked into lines; tiny pale shells that crunch underfoot; salt rime on the hut posts; a single faded orange cone; wind ripples in a thin dust skin; an oil-drum lane marker lying on its side.

**Sound events:** crust ticking and pinging as the temperature drops, which is how the night sounds here; boards creaking in the hut; the flag cloth; a single gust that dies at once.

**Evening:** pink dusk on pale ground, a mirage band at the horizon that is gone within a minute, then moon on white. From the road a lit lantern is a spark in the nothing, visible for a very long way.

**Considered and rejected:** a lit sign (a lit window defeats the dark), a mirror-lake reflection (theatre), animals (the land is empty of them).

| Hollowed log | Walk through. The sound closes around you; the fog is a bright circle at each end. | A cathedral of one tree. |

**Ground and small traces:** wet bay leaves; moss hanging from oak limbs; the white lines of the road fading at the edges of the headlights; a rain-swollen seed catalogue in a mailbox; drips from every eave; fog beading on wire; a flattened cardboard sign; redwood duff soft underfoot.

**Sound events:** irregular drips from a roof edge; the radiator tick; the diner's door spring; wind dropping in steps as you descend; tyres on wet pavement softening everything.

**Evening:** above the fog the sky has a pink band and the cloud deck is a bright floor; below it, grey; at night the headlights make a cone of grain and nothing else.

**Considered and rejected:** figures in the fog, ghost lights, flickering signs for tension. All of those are theatre.

*The road ends where the land does. There is no horizon light. You have come as far as anyone can.*

**Set pieces**

| Structure | What it is | What it evokes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dark lighthouse | A tower with an empty lantern room that catches moonlight | The beam is absent. The light keeper is a memory. |

| Guest register | Turn pages. Entries end at an old date; the last line is half filled. | A stay that never finished. |

| Ice machine | Lift the lid. A drained basin with a rim of salt. | A comfort meant for travellers. |
| Porch chair | Sit facing the sea. A creak, and the surf at an even rhythm. | Waiting with the whole ocean. |
| Glass fishing float | At the beach, pick one up from the tide line. Carry it; set it down. | A thing the sea carried a very long way to leave here. |
| Bluff stair | Walk down. The surf grows. The sand is wet at the tide line. | The last steps of a long drive. |

**Ground and small traces:** sand drifting across the lot; salt pitting on the signs and window frames; driftwood logs; strands of kelp; wet rock; dune grass flattened by the wind; rust running down the railings; a pair of boots' worth of footprints that are your own.

**Sound events:** surf as a slow pulse; sign hum shifting in the wind; a rope creaking on a post; a window rattling in its frame; a rare gull-free hush.

**Evening:** pearl grey and rose, a sea that gives back no light, and an empty horizon at night with no ships or buoys.

**Considered and rejected:** a sweeping lighthouse beam (it is theatre and implies a keeper), ships' lights, a foghorn, gulls.

**Considered and rejected:** a destination, a lit house, boats (a lift bridge with no boats is stronger).

## Open decisions raised here

- **Projector (Great Plains).** A lit blank screen is the strongest image in the region and the closest thing to spectacle. Decide whether a player-operated, plain white beam fits the restraint rule.
- **The lantern (Salt playa).** Lighting a lamp is not a campfire, but confirm it is acceptable as the one exception to the one-warm-pool grammar.
- **Fireflies (Farm belt).** Optional, few, silent. Confirm whether they count as sparse insects.
- **Speaker field sound (Plains).** Confirm that a hung speaker carrying filtered wind, with no music, fits the no-music rule.
- **Penny scale text (Lakes).** The card text is placeholder; decide whether any fortune-style text belongs.
- **Naming.** All names here are working names. The Plains drive-in is now Harvest Moon Drive-In, so it does not echo Starlite Diner.
- **Set pieces and WORLD_LORE.** Confirm that non-enterable set pieces do not count against the "one new visitable place per region" rule.
