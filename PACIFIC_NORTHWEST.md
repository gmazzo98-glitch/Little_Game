# Pacific Northwest expansion proposal

This proposal covers a future playable region and a continuous world clock. [AESTHETIC.md](AESTHETIC.md) governs mood and lighting, [WORLD_LORE.md](WORLD_LORE.md) describes the existing stops, and [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) tracks delivery and acceptance. The proposal replaces the five-minute night hold with a full cycle when implemented. The playable build currently uses the night hold.

## The drive

**Cinder Court Motor Lodge is the last desert stop and the starting point for the new drive.** It already sits 4.6 km west of Sunset Service. From the motel, continue west to a signed state-road fork, then northwest into a river valley. Keep Sunset Service at the center of the desert map and Starlite Diner & Bar 4.6 km east of it. The diner stays an eastbound destination. Fern Bend is a small roadside stand with a river and forest to explore, distinct in scale and purpose from Starlite's diner and lounge.

The attachment sets the scale for the arrival. A low roadside ice-cream stand sits under an immense, dark ridge, with one warm sign and a few lit windows. Use that relationship for a fictional 1960s stand called **Fern Bend Drive-In**. Keep its painted timber, amber service window, and small cone sign low in the frame. Across the road, the wooded ridge should rise through two or three muted layers and vanish into low cloud. The stand feels like a place the road used to pass through every day. The empty lot and sun-faded menu tell that story.

Take the geographic cue from the Columbia River Gorge, where a river corridor connects arid grasslands and wet forests across a strong moisture gradient. The expansion compresses that kind of journey into a fictional route. Give the fork its own state-road signs and let Route 66 continue west. [U.S. Forest Service regional overview](https://www.fs.usda.gov/pnw/pubs/pnw_gtr1001.pdf)

| Driving distance west from Cinder Court | Road and landscape | What the player notices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 km | Open basin beyond the motel | Keep the first few kilometers recognizably desert. Cinder Court disappears behind the coupe. Low ridges gain definition, cacti thin out, and tawny grasses fill the washes. |
| 5–11 km | Northwest fork and dry foothills | The straight highway continues west while the state road begins a broad climb. The first ponderosa pines occupy sheltered gullies, with bare rock and grass still visible between them. Sand gives way to gravel in the shoulder. |
| 11–18 km | Dry ridge and descent into the gorge | Look back into the open basin from one bend and down into the wooded valley from the next. Keep the sunny face across the valley dry while trees climb the shaded side. The river starts as a distant sound. |
| 18–23 km | Gorge, bridge, and riverside pullout | Damp streaks appear below rock cuts. Alders and denser conifers follow the water. The river becomes visible through gaps in the trees. The bridge gives the player an unhurried view up the gorge. |
| 23–30 km | Fern Bend valley, with the drive-in around km 26 | Tall conifers, fern banks, wet asphalt, low cloud, and intermittent rain. Mist sometimes settles over the river while the ridge stays visible in layers. The canopy light and cone sign emerge late, around a bend. |
| Beyond 30 km | Through road along the river and out through wooded ridges | Long quiet stretches, changing views of water and ridges, occasional practical pullouts, and mileposts that keep counting. |

Measure these prototype distances along the driven road, beginning at the Cinder Court turnoff. At an assumed average of 75–100 km/h, Fern Bend is roughly **16–21 minutes from Cinder Court** or **18–25 minutes from Sunset Service**, before stops. Time ordinary drives in the coupe and tune the empty stretches around the bends, grades, and pullouts.

Let the geography carry the transition even on a clear day. A continuous terrain profile takes the road from the basin floor into foothills and then down to the river. Use slope, shade, drainage, and distance from the river to overlap soil and vegetation, with dry grass on exposed slopes and damp plants filling gullies sooner. One bend can look back toward dusty rock while the next frames the dark forest ahead. Bring the wooded skyline into view several bends before reaching it. Fade the far desert silhouettes into distant foothills where terrain can conceal the handoff. Let the tires move from dry pavement to occasional damp strips under shade before the valley road becomes broadly wet. On the return, the same bends open up the sky again. The river, the road grade, and the trees should still tell the story with sound muted or weather set to clear.

## Explore Fern Bend on foot

- **Drive-in and lot.** Give the coupe a pull-through entrance, parking beside the canopy, and room to turn. Walk around the building and through a side door into a modest counter room. Read the old menu and look back through rain-streaked glass toward the ridge. Under the canopy, the rain changes from a steady wash to drops at the gutter.
- **River access.** A gravel lane leads to a day-use pullout and a short path down to the bank. A footbridge over a side creek and a return path under the trees make a loop back to the lot. Leave breaks in the trees to keep the river and the stand useful as landmarks.
- **Ridge pullout.** Farther down the main road, a turnout and an uphill footpath reach a viewpoint over the bridge, the stand roof, and the forest. Connect the viewpoint back to the valley footpath so the player can walk the whole area and return to the parked coupe.

Build those places as one connected landscape, with walkable shoulders, forest floor, banks, and openings wherever the terrain looks traversable. Use believable grades, water, and steep rock to shape routes. Give each turnout clear entry and re-entry in either direction. Let wet gravel, runoff, and period fittings carry the detail. Keep the road empty and leave long stretches of darkness between the few warm fixtures.

## Terrain, weather, and distance

- **One road surface from desert to forest.** Reuse the existing continuous terrain, player-centered recentering, road shading, and driving controls. Keep the existing straight segment as the first road alignment. Add a smooth centerline and grade profile for the branch. Use the same road geometry to shape the ground, lay out asphalt and paint, classify tires, position poles and signs, and connect bridge decks, shoulders, and aprons. Check the branch junction and steepest section with both the coupe and the walking controller.
- **Blend in world space.** Sample the same terrain and moisture fields from the road and on foot. Use stable positions for vegetation and interpolate surface and weather inputs across segment boundaries. Read climate from location rather than mileage traveled, so approaching from another direction, stopping, or turning around gives coherent conditions.
- **Keep the main roads open for long sessions.** Author the fork, pass, and Fern Bend by hand. Beyond the authored valley, extend the main road in deterministic segments, with varied bends, grades, ridge profiles, and river setbacks. Generate ahead of the coupe and recycle distant scenery in both directions. Use stable segment IDs and positions so a U-turn brings back the same roadside, and retain the coupe and local interaction state when the player leaves on foot or drives back from far away. Keep render and physics coordinates near the player if long-distance tests expose precision loss. The extended road has continuous mileage, with no finish gate, automatic turnaround, or fuel requirement that strands the player. Local side roads can end at a place with room to turn.
- **Budget for the wet night drive.** Batch conifers, rocks, and undergrowth by region. Stream nearby obstacles and their collisions together. Keep rain, lights, and rendered terrain bounded as distance grows. Use the existing offline `index.html` and bundled Three.js, with all assets embedded. Follow [ASSET_GUIDE.md](ASSET_GUIDE.md) for close-view models, material budgets, and provenance.

## Forest assets and weather

**Build trees around their silhouettes.** Give foothill ponderosas exposed trunks, irregular open crowns, and room for light between branches. Around Fern Bend, build taller Douglas-firs with layered boughs, western hemlocks with drooping leaders, and western redcedars with broad, fibrous bases in sheltered damp pockets. Start with two or three distinct crown and trunk shapes per main conifer. Use roots that meet the actual slope, broken lower limbs, dark branch undersides, and uneven gaps through the crown. Reserve the best trunks, root flares, and boughs for the bank, footbridge, and viewpoint. Group younger trees around gaps and mossy nurse logs. Keep sword ferns, moss, stones, and wet duff concentrated around the places the player can approach. Use muted evergreen, gray bark, rust, and a few damp greens in the existing painterly style. [North Cascades dry-slope life zones](https://www.nps.gov/noca/learn/nature/bird-life-zones.htm) · [Olympic lowland forests](https://home.nps.gov/olym/learn/nature/lowland-forests.htm) · [Olympic rainforest understory and nurse logs](https://www.nps.gov/olym/learn/nature/temperate-rain-forests.htm)

Use shaped foliage clusters and opaque trunks close to the path. Simplify the same crown shapes into instanced mid-distance trees and irregular canopy silhouettes along the ridges. Fade detail with distance and preserve the outline as trees switch models. Check the stand of trees from the coupe at speed and from arm's length in daylight, when bark, branch attachments, and repeated silhouettes are easiest to spot. At the drive-in, give the cone sign a shaped housing and readable tubing, the service window a real recess and frame, and the canopy an underside, gutter, and believable posts. The building and foreground trees should read by shape in dry daylight as well as in rain at night.

**Give rain, surface wetness, and fog separate controls.** Let the valley drift between overcast, drizzle, and breaks. Puddles collect in low spots and asphalt dries gradually after rain eases. Use broken, angle-dependent highlights from the headlights and the drive-in window. Put fine moving rain in the headlight beam and a few readable drips at eaves. Keep fog low over water and hollows, with occasional thicker patches, gradual visibility falloff, and layered ridge silhouettes. Keep lane edges readable at the foggiest supported setting. Let cloud cover soften daylight and mute stars or moonlight at night. Keep effects local and bounded, with subtle glass streaks from inside the coupe and rain occluded under roofs. Preserve the quiet camera and restrained light pools.

**Let the sound change with the same landscape.** Keep open desert wind audible beyond the motel. As the road climbs, let exposed gusts give way to softer air through pines. The river enters as a distant, directional sound from below the grade, grows around the bridge, then falls away as the road climbs. Crossfade tire noise by actual surface and speed, from dry grit to a soft wet hiss. In the parked coupe, hear a muted patter on the roof and glass. Outside, hear wider rain and the river. Under the drive-in canopy, hear discrete gutter drips. Indoors, muffle the rain and place it at the window and roof. Use the actual door and shelter boundaries, with short crossfades and stable sources as scenery streams. Keep the mix sparse and procedural, preserve mute, and review the same route with the engine running and shut off. Quiet intervals should feel as deliberate as the rain.

## A continuous clock and a place to rest

Start a fresh game at late dusk and keep the existing roughly five-minute path into full dark. Then let night pass into dawn, day, and dusk again everywhere on the map. Prototype a **48-minute real-time day** with an **18:30** start and full dark near **21:00**. That gives a normal no-stop drive from Sunset a possible late-night or early-dawn arrival. Tune by playing whole drives. Keep the first dusk's warm desert horizon and cold night as reference views. Use pale daylight in the valley, shaded greens under the trees, and soft cloud light on the road. The clock runs while driving, walking, or parked. Track one advancing world time, with a repeating solar phase and a separate weather timeline. Feed sky, light direction, stars, fog color, and ambient light from that phase so midnight and sunrise stay continuous at every location.

Reuse the existing wrap-up blanket in the parked coupe and add a rest action to the furnished bed in Cinder Court's Room 1. Let the player settle first and look out the windshield or across the room. Rest stays optional. A second deliberate action, **Rest until…**, shows a small period-style alarm-clock dial, framed as a dash clock in the coupe or a bedside clock in Room 1. Offer **First light**, **Noon**, **Blue hour**, and **Midnight** as quick choices, plus a clock setting for a desired hour and minute. Show the next occurrence and how many hours will pass before confirming. Cancel returns to the same view. Enable coupe rest only when stopped with the parking brake set and the blanket wrapped. Use the same time controls at the bed.

On confirmation, fade the view and soften the cabin or room sound for a few seconds, advance time to the selected point, then fade back into the same parked coupe or motel room. Keep the player's position, the coupe, the blanket, and the state of doors, lamps, and drawers. Update weather and surface wetness to the wake-up time so the view and audio agree when they return. Keep passing hours forward across midnight, including when the chosen hour is earlier on the dial. A player who wants to see the forest wake up can set the bedside clock for an early departure. A player who wants the sign, rain, and headlights can leave after dark or rest in the coupe at a turnout until blue hour comes around again.

## Keep the glovebox map current

Keep the **1963 Western Motor Guide** as paper in the coupe, readable while stopped using the existing glovebox and map actions. Retain the desert overview with Sunset Service, Cinder Court to the west, and Starlite Diner to the east. Use Cinder Court as the shared reference point on the new regional panel, which shows the northwest fork, pass, bridge, Fern Bend, and both turnouts, with a small inset for the walking loop. Mark the regional route's name or shield at the fork, match it to the roadside sign in both travel directions, and put a continuation arrow and milepost range where the highway leaves the printed panel. Players can orient from junctions, landmarks and mileposts even when they're beyond the authored part of the map.

`buildRoadMap()` currently draws the three stops from hard-coded labels and positions. Replace that duplication during implementation with one inline route catalog. Give each named route, junction and visitable stop a stable ID, road position, printed label, connections, and mileage along the road. Use that catalog for scene placement, signs, the map canvas, and the office mileage strip. Keep simulated distance and existing period mileage labels as separate fields, so the printed 18-mile labels retain their meaning and mileage within each new signed route increases consistently.

Treat the map as part of every road or destination change. A playable turnoff, moved junction, or renamed stop ships with the corresponding map panel, road signs and applicable mileage strip updated in the same change. Check that every printed turnoff exists and can be driven, that every authored public destination has a map entry, and that mileposts agree with the route ordering. Check legibility under the cabin lamp. The paper map uses stable route and distance cues, so it remains useful during a long drive without a live location marker.

## Build order and acceptance

1. **Close the open graphics foundation checks in the roadmap.** Capture the existing first dusk at all three desert stops and use it as the lighting regression baseline. Keep offline play and normal controls in the baseline too.
2. **Prove the drive in graybox.** Build the fork, continuous road profile, river crossing, valley loop, and first streamed continuation. Test with matching signs and map. Drive both ways from Cinder Court, turn around beyond Fern Bend, and check that Route 66 and the new through road both remain open. Check the Starlite route and turnoffs in the same build.
3. **Prove the clock and both rest actions at existing stops.** Observe a full natural cycle through dusk twice. Try a same-day wake time, a skip over midnight, cancel, and repeated rests at the bed and in the coupe. Check that control returns at the same place, the room and car keep their state, and the sky and weather agree at wake-up.
4. **Finish Fern Bend and the transition.** Review one foreground grove, a mid-distance stand, and a distant ridge before filling the valley. Build the full walking loop and drive-in interior, then tune the weather and sound in motion. Capture matching road views at dawn, overcast day, dusk, and night in both directions. Repeat in clear weather, drizzle, and river fog. Listen at the open basin, pine slope, bridge, parked coupe, canopy, and counter room. Check for visible forest bands, model pops, rain under cover, abrupt sound transitions, and lost lane edges.
5. **Verify the delivered file.** Follow the roadmap's inline syntax, resource scan, and offline `file://` checks. Time a normal trip from Cinder Court. Walk the loop, enter and exit the drive-in, find the coupe again, and read the glovebox map. Drive at least 30 minutes beyond the valley, then turn around and return, checking repeatable landmarks, bounded memory and scenery counts, and frame-time spikes at segment changes. Measure sustained wet-night driving and on-foot exploration on the named mid-range laptop, recording hardware, viewport, pixel ratio, median and p95 frame times against the 60 fps target. Record any missing evidence before claiming acceptance.
